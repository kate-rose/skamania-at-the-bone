Skamania at the bone

A briefing for Representatives-elect Ben Christly and Diana Perez, 17th Legislative District

$545,000

The gap still open in Skamania County's 2027 general fund when commissioners adopted the preliminary budget on Nov. 3, 2026. Meeting recording

The final budget and the 2027 property-tax levies are set at a public hearing on Dec. 15, 2026, at 5 p.m., Courthouse Room 18, Stevenson.

The one-minute version

  1. The county's general property tax can grow 1% a year under state law, plus what new construction and a few other things add. Its general-fund operating costs, as filed with the State Auditor, grew 5 to 6% a year over the last decade. That gap widens every year on its own.
  2. 88% of the county's land is exempt from property tax, most of it national forest. Secure Rural Schools, the federal payment that replaced the county's share of national-forest timber sales, is down 76% from its 2006 peak, before inflation.
  3. One-time money helped hold the 2025 and 2026 budgets together, including $2.5 million of federal relief set aside for county insurance premiums. None of it is ongoing.
  4. In October and November 2026, departments were asked to cut 8%, hiring was put on hold, and the Sheriff's Office offered to halve its overtime.
  5. Several of the county's asks in Olympia were carried by Rep. Kevin Waters, whose House term ends in January 2027 and who also runs the Skamania County Economic Development Council. Some need a new carrier in the 2027 session. They're listed below.
It will be 2 a.m., deputies go home, and then good luck calling 911.

The Sheriff's Office, arguing against deeper cuts to its own budget at the county's budget workshop, Oct. 27, 2026. Meeting recording

1 · How deep it is

The cushion is thinning while the gap stays open

The county keeps a cash cushion in its general fund for bad years. By its own reserve table, that cushion peaked at the end of 2023 and fell in each of the next two years. On Nov. 3, 2026 the board adopted a new rule: keep at least 12% of five-year average spending in reserve.

The overtime, I kind of feel like it's a little bit of fool's gold.

A commissioner, on the Sheriff's Office offer to halve overtime, Nov. 3, 2026. The commissioner later called the phrase "a little bit harsh." Search-and-rescue calls can't be scheduled away. On Oct. 27 the Sheriff's Office had called a 50% cut "not realistic based on just the numbers this year," adding, "I'll do it." Meeting recording

2 · Why this isn't one bad year

Two blades closing

One blade is state law: the county's regular property tax can grow 1% a year, plus what new construction and a few other things add. The other is cost: wages, insurance, fuel and state-set standards grow faster. Skamania also has very little to tax.

What's also fair to say

  • Skamania isn't poor on every measure. In 2022 it collected $1,210 per resident in its own revenue, about 1.46 times the national county median and seventh of 39 Washington counties. A small county carries fixed costs (a jail, a courthouse, a sheriff) over few people, so that number reads high.
  • The board chose not to take its 1% for 2025. It banked the $26,605, which it can still claim in a later year.
  • Some costs are local choices, such as the new finance software. In October 2026 the county reported its payroll module would not be ready before April 2027. Meeting recording
  • Skamania counts as metro in federal statistics because it's in the Portland-Vancouver area. Programs keyed to "rural" may not see it that way.

4 · What the state controls

Six levers in Olympia's hands

Most of what squeezes Skamania's general fund is set in state law, the state budget or a state court rule. Each of these has a real tradeoff; we've tried to state both sides.

The 1% levy limit

What it is
RCW 84.55 caps how much a county's regular property-tax revenue can grow each year: 1% over the highest of the last three levies, plus add-ons for new construction, improvements and state-assessed property. For a county of 10,000 people or more, the 1% drops to the inflation rate in a low-inflation year unless two of three commissioners find "substantial need," which restores up to 1% for that year. Going higher takes a voter-approved lid lift.
What Skamania sees
The 1% was worth $27,086.77 to the general fund for 2026; the levy resolution passed unanimously. No county lid lift is on record.
What's been tried
HB 1334 and SB 5798 (2025) would have tied the limit to inflation plus population. Neither passed. ESHB 2442 (2026) kept the 1% but allows lid lifts of up to 10 years (in effect since July 1, 2026), lets counties split off the veterans and mental-health levies, adds a small public-health-clinic levy, and lets rental-car tax pay for public defenders. Its property-tax sections apply to taxes collected in 2027, including the levy set Dec. 15, 2026, so 2027 levy figures won't line up with earlier years.
The tradeoff
A looser cap lets revenue keep pace with costs, and it raises property-tax bills for people already stretched. A tight cap keeps bills predictable, and it pushes counties toward cuts, fees, levy elections and dependence on transfers.

RCW 84.55.005 · RCW 84.55.0101 · ESHB 2442 · HB 1334 · SB 5798

Public defense

What it is
Counties pay for lawyers for people who can't afford one. In 2025 the state Supreme Court cut the felony caseload limit from 150 cases per lawyer a year to 47 weighted case credits, effective Jan. 1, 2026, with up to ten years to phase in. The state's grant helps; the rest is the county's.
What Skamania sees
On Nov. 10, 2026 the board approved advertising for a Superior Court defender for 2027 because one attorney can't return. Staff warned on Oct. 27 that the public-defense line "could probably go up." Meeting recording
What's been tried
HB 1592 and SB 5404 (2025), as introduced, would have had the state pay half of trial-level costs and let counties under 50 people per square mile ask the state Office of Public Defense to take over the work; Skamania has about 7. Neither passed. SB 5914 (a phased state share) was introduced; ESSB 5912 (a task force) passed the Senate 48–0 and stalled.
The tradeoff
A state share eases the county's bill and costs the state general fund. Leaving it local keeps control local, and leaves small counties competing for scarce lawyers.

State forest land and DNR's share

What it is
DNR manages forest land the county gave the state, sells the timber and keeps a management share before paying the county. RCW 79.64.110 caps that share at 25%, with written authority for DNR's board to go to 27% that each biennial budget bill renews. The current authority runs through June 30, 2027, so extending it is a 2027-session decision. Separately, the capital budget pays Skamania for state forest land locked up under endangered-species rules.
What Skamania sees
DNR's board scheduled the 25% to 27% increase for its Nov. 3, 2026 meeting. A commissioner put the cost at $63,000 to $69,000 a year on a ten-year average. Commissioners describe the locked-up-land payment as about $1.8 million a biennium and worry it will move from the capital to the operating budget. Meeting recording
From the record
"$60,000 for us is considerable considering that we're probably going to lose our veteran services officer." A commissioner, Oct. 20, 2026. No decision on that position is on record. Meeting recording
The tradeoff
Funding DNR's management account from the state general fund spares timber counties and competes with everything else. Raising the fee moves DNR's shortfall onto rural counties and their junior districts.

RCW 79.64.110 · State Forest Land Replacement

Forest money and school funding

What it is
Congress sets Secure Rural Schools. The state decides what happens next: under RCW 28A.520.020, federal forest money a school district receives is offset against its state basic-education funding, except that districts with 57% or more low-income students keep the first $70,000. The 2023–25 budget suspended the offset.
What Skamania sees
A payment that arrived after the June 30, 2025 window cost Stevenson-Carson School District roughly $250,000 to $300,000, by the account given to commissioners. Rep. Waters said on June 23, 2026 that he had a bill drafted to fix it. The state schools office has said the Legislature would need to restore the budget language or amend the statute. Meeting recording
The tradeoff
Holding forest districts harmless costs the state school budget and gives those districts more per student than others. Not doing it means a federal payment meant for rural schools partly replaces state money instead of adding to it.

RCW 28A.520.020

Foundational public health

What it is
The state funds a core set of local public-health services through Foundational Public Health Services money (RCW 43.70.515).
What Skamania sees
The county received $1,076,000 against $1,369,000 expected, about $293,000 less, and held a position open while waiting on state decisions. Klickitat's health department reported losing about $380,000. Meeting recording
The tradeoff
Restoring the money competes in a tight state budget. Not restoring it shifts core work like disease investigation onto county general funds whose main tax grows about 1% a year plus new construction.

RCW 43.70.515

Mandates with deadlines

What it is
The Growth Management Act's periodic review names Skamania County and its cities for Dec. 31, 2026 (RCW 36.70A.130). Because Skamania doesn't fully plan under the act, its review covers critical areas and resource lands, and small, slow-growing counties and cities may take up to 24 more months. RCW 43.135.060 says the state must pay for new mandates; its text doesn't reach court rules, funding cuts or agency fees.
What Skamania sees
The county's critical-areas ordinance was on the Nov. 10, 2026 agenda for adoption, after state fish and ecology agencies objected to its stream buffers. Stevenson asked about a one-year extension. North Bonneville and Bingen have their own deadlines in the same cycle. Meeting recording
The tradeoff
Uniform state standards protect shared resources. Small jurisdictions carry the same paperwork as large ones with a fraction of the staff.
